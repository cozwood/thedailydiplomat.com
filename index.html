Beijing Reasserts Tech Sovereignty While Managing Foreign Capital, As North Korea-Russia Infrastructure Ties Deepen And Pacific Alliance Realignment Accelerates

China is executing a calibrated recalibration of its foreign technology investment posture following the Meta-Manus blockade, signaling willingness to accept foreign capital while maintaining state control over strategically sensitive AI and data assets. This stance reflects Beijing's confidence in sectoral gatekeeping mechanisms—regulatory review, security screening, and indirect pressure on domestic firms—rather than blunt capital controls. Simultaneously, North Korea's constitutional revisions and accelerating infrastructure integration with Russia (bridge construction, nuclear cooperation) signal a hardening of the Moscow-Pyongyang axis that bypasses Beijing's traditional mediation role. In the Pacific, Solomon Islands leadership transitions and U.S. Taiwan arms delays reveal fault lines in both superpower alliance management and the timing of great power competition.

The theater faces competing pressures: China managing optics of economic openness while consolidating state power over critical technologies; Russia-North Korea coordination moving forward independent of Chinese preference; the United States balancing Taiwan commitment against Middle Eastern obligations; and secondary powers (India, South Korea, Solomon Islands) navigating multipolar constraints with eroding room for hedging.

Developments of Note
01.
**China - Tech Investment Gatekeeping**: Beijing's explicit reassurance on foreign technology investment follows the Meta-Manus rejection, indicating China will permit foreign capital acquisition in non-sensitive sectors while deploying regulatory opacity and indirect state pressure to block foreign control of AI startups and data infrastructure. This two-tier approach preserves openness narrative while maintaining actual sovereignty over the technological frontier.
